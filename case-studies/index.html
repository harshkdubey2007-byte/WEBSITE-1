Key takeaways

5 lessons from 35 brands

The same few ideas came up again and again in these 35 stories. Remember these five and you have the heart of every case study above.

  1. 1
    Educate before you sell

    Answer the questions people already have, and trust follows.

    Seen inNykaaHubSpotThe Little Farm Co.
  2. 2
    Real customers are your best ads

    Videos, photos and reviews from real people convince more than polished ads.

    Seen inGoProMamaearthNykaa
  3. 3
    Make the first step small

    A cheap trial or a daily offer gets people started; follow-ups do the rest.

    Seen inPerfume brandMcDonald’sZyra Jewel
  4. 4
    Join the conversation

    Talk about what people are already discussing, in a voice they enjoy.

    Seen inAmulZomatoHeinz
  5. 5
    Measure, then fix the basics

    Good tracking and clear goals make every rupee of ads work harder.

    Seen inThe North FaceL’OréalSEVEN

About these case studies

What is a case study in digital marketing?

A case study is a short, real story of how a brand solved a marketing problem: what was going wrong, what the team did, and what changed as a result. It turns marketing theory into something you can see working.

Are these brands eBuddha Digitech’s clients?

No. These are independent case studies of well-known brands, written for learning. eBuddha Digitech is not linked to or endorsed by these brands, and all brand names and logos belong to their owners.

Where do the numbers and facts come from?

From public reports, brand announcements, award entries and marketing publications. Every story has a Source link at the end of its full story, and figures from third parties are marked “reported” or “estimate”.

How are these case studies organised?

There are 35 stories in 11 topics, grouped under Foundations, Brand & Creative, Ads & Direct Marketing, and Search & Websites. Each card shows the problem and the result first, and opens into the full story.

For students & beginners

Can a complete beginner understand these case studies?

Yes. Each card is marked Beginner or Intermediate, the language is kept simple, and words like ROAS, UGC or SEO have a dotted underline: tap one to see what it means.

Which case study should I read first?

Start with the cards marked Start here: Nykaa, Amul, Zomato, Red Bull, Nike and The Little Farm Co. They are short, easy to follow and cover the basics of strategy, content, social media and branding.

Can I use these case studies for college assignments or projects?

Yes, as a starting point. Read the story, open its source link for the full details, and then write your own analysis in your own words. Always mention the original source in your assignment.

Can I download these case studies as a PDF?

There is no PDF download yet. You can save or print this page as a PDF from your browser (Print, then “Save as PDF”), and each story links to its original source.

Learning & career

How are these case studies used in the eBuddha Digitech course?

Each module of our Digital Marketing Course in Bhopal includes the case studies that match its topic. Learners discuss them in class and then apply the same ideas in their own projects.

How do I write my own marketing case study?

Use the same four parts as these cards: the problem (what was going wrong), what you did (the steps), the result (numbers if you have them), and the lesson. Keep it short and honest.

Can case studies help me get a marketing job?

Yes. Recruiters like candidates who can explain why a campaign worked. Adding two or three of your own case studies to your portfolio, written in this format, shows you can think like a marketer, not just use tools.

What do interviewers ask about marketing case studies?

Common questions are: “Explain a campaign you admire and why it worked”, “Which numbers would you track?”, and “What would you do differently?”. The problem, steps, result and lesson on each card help you answer these clearly.

Across these 35 stories, the same things repeat: a clear audience, one simple idea, the right channel, a small first step for the customer, and careful measurement. See “5 lessons from 35 brands” near the top of this page.
